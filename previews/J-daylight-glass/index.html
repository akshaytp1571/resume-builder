How it works

From paste to polished.

Four things happen between a job posting and a résumé a machine can read. Scroll through them.

Step 01

Paste the posting.

Drop in any job description. We read the whole thing — the requirements, the responsibilities, and the exact language they use for the work.

Step 02

We parse your résumé.

Every section, date, and bullet becomes structured data a machine can actually read. Anything the parser can't place gets flagged rather than silently dropped.

Step 03

Then we score it.

The score reads you the way a machine does. We extract each section, match its language against the posting, and weight four things: parse fidelity, keyword coverage, formatting risk, and seniority match. A 91 means you're legible — with a few gaps worth closing.

Step 04

Tailor and export.

Rewrite the weak bullets, keep your voice, and export clean plain text or a typeset PDF that reads well to machines and people alike.

The score

How the number is built.

Four weighted criteria, each computed from text you can point at. Nothing here is a vibe.

parse_fidelity 94

How much of your résumé the parser can read into clean fields. Tables, text boxes, floating graphics, and odd spacing all cost you here — the machine has to guess where your experience ends.

keyword_coverage 88

The share of the posting's required terms that appear in your résumé, matched on meaning rather than exact strings. "Cut deploy time" counts for "deployment velocity".

formatting_risk 12

How much your layout is likely to break on the way through a plain-text pass. Lower is better — multi-column bodies, icons, and header tables are the usual culprits.

seniority_match 90

Whether your titles, dates, and scope read at the level the posting is hiring for. A staff posting wants to see ownership, breadth, and numbers at scale.

No black box. Every point traces to a line in your résumé — click any criterion and the studio shows you the exact sentence it came from.

FAQ

The short answers.

Does it upload my résumé?

No. Parsing, scoring, and export all run in this tab. Your résumé is written to localStorage on your machine and never sent anywhere. The only optional network calls are the ones you turn on yourself — an AI provider with your own key, or the local job agent you start.

What is an ATS score?

A read on how a machine sees your résumé, not a verdict on your career. It measures whether the parser can find your sections, whether the posting's language shows up in your text, and whether your formatting survives a plain-text pass. A high score gets you read; it does not get you hired.

Can I use my own AI key?

Yes. Bring a key from a provider you already pay for and it's stored in your browser. Nothing is proxied through us, and the studio works exactly the same with it switched off — rewriting bullets is a convenience, not a dependency.

Is it really free?

Yes. MIT licensed, no account, no trial, no paid tier hiding the export. If you'd rather run it yourself, the whole thing is a static build — clone it, serve the folder, and it works offline.
