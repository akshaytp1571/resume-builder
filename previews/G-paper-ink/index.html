Most résumés are not read badly — they are read partially. A parser pulls a flat stream of text out of your PDF and then guesses. Columns get interleaved, dates drift away from the jobs they belong to, and a stray bullet can orphan an entire role. Résumé Studio grades three things instead of vibes: parse fidelity (did every field survive the trip), keyword coverage (does the posting's own language appear where a machine looks), and formatting risk (tables, text boxes, headers and footers that quietly swallow half your history).

02 —

Scoring

Four numbers, recomputed on every keystroke. No letter grades, no green ticks — just the four things a screening system actually weighs.

  • Parse fidelity 94

    How much of your document arrives as structured, correctly-attributed text. Anything above 90 means the parser is not guessing.

  • Keyword coverage 71

    The share of the posting's required terms that appear in your summary, titles and bullets — weighted by where they appear, not just whether.

  • Formatting risklower is better 12

    Layout features known to break extraction: multi-column bodies, tables, text boxes, images of text, and content parked in headers or footers.

  • Seniority match 88

    Whether your titles, scope and years line up with the level the posting is hiring for — the filter that quietly screens people out before anyone reads a word.

03 —

Templates

Three, and no more. Each one is a single column of real text with a hairline rule structure — the kind of page a parser reads top to bottom without improvising.

Editorial

A serif name, ruled sections, generous margins. Reads like a journal article and gives a long career room to breathe.

1 column · serif display

Ledger

A narrow date rail beside a dense body column. Tight leading, twenty years of history, one page.

2 columns · rail dates

Plain

One typeface, no rules, no colour. The safest thing you can hand a parser, and the one we reach for first.

1 column · no decoration

05 —

Private by construction

Your résumé never leaves this browser.

There is no account to make and no server to trust. Every keystroke is written to this tab's localStorage and nowhere else — close the laptop, reopen it tomorrow, and the draft is exactly where you left it. Export happens on your machine, in your font, on your paper.

  • No accountNothing to sign up for, nothing to recover, no password to forget.
  • No uploadYour file is opened with the File Reader API. It is read, never sent.
  • No telemetryNo analytics script, no error beacon, no third-party request of any kind.
06 —

The job agent

Optional, off by default, and entirely local. The job agent is a small Playwright script you run yourself: point it at a posting, and it reads the requirements back to you as plain text.

From there it can draft bullet rewrites in your own voice — but it is a power-user feature, not a magic bullet. It will not invent experience you do not have, and it will not apply to anything on your behalf. Read the posting, accept the edits you like, discard the rest.

If you want a model in the loop, bring your own key. Nothing is proxied through us.

  • OpenAI
  • Local Ollama
  • No model at all
