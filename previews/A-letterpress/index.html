The argument

Eight seconds, then a machine

You have eight seconds. Maybe six. A recruiter reads in a Z — top left, across, down the right — hunting a shape they already know. Before the human arrives, a parser reads your headings, your columns, your dates, and files what it can find.

Most résumé tools want you inside a database. An account. A server. A row with your name on it. Then they charge you to get your own career back out as a PDF.

Resume Studio is one page of HTML that thinks. The ATS score moves while you type. Paste the posting and the missing keywords light up. Switch to parser view and read the same document the way the machine reads it: orphaned headings, swallowed tables, a date format nobody taught it.

Close the tab and nothing happened to us. There is no us. There is a file, and it is yours.

Index of parts

The whole instrument,
in six movements

  1. 01

    ATS score

    A number out of one hundred, with the reasons printed underneath. It never guesses, and it never flatters.

  2. 02

    Keyword match

    Paste the posting. The words you are missing get underlined against the words you have. That is the whole feature.

  3. 03

    Parser view

    Your document as applicant tracking software extracts it. Usually ugly. Now you can see where the table went.

  4. 04

    Cover letters

    Same type, same margins, same spot ink as the résumé. A letter is not an afterthought stapled to a PDF.

  5. 05

    Application ledger

    Company, role, date sent, silence. A ruled ledger, not a kanban board with fourteen columns.

  6. 06

    Apply agent

    Opens the form, fills what it already knows, stops before submit. You press the last button. Always.
